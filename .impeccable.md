## Design Context

### Users
A small, closed group of friends (Dima, Yossi, Moti, Shai, Tofchi, Eli, ~a handful of people) predicting World Cup 2026 match outcomes against each other. Everyone knows everyone; this is a friendly, ongoing competition, not a public product. Users open the app on their own initiative (pull, not push) — there is no notification/digest delivery model to design for, everything is discovered inside the app.

### Brand Personality
"Professional like a sports broadcast, festive and impressive." Think official FIFA broadcast graphics and trophy presentation — polished, dramatic, celebratory — rather than a plain admin dashboard. Confident and a little theatrical, but still legible and fast for quick daily check-ins.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Theme: dark navy (#030d1a base) with gold accents (#FFD700 / #f5c518), consistent across the whole app.
- Display font: 'Russo One' for the big "World Cup" wordmark treatment; 'Syne' for tracked-out numerals/labels (e.g. "2026"); 'Outfit' as the body font.
- Motion: framer-motion for entrance choreography (staggered fades/springs), animated trophy glow, floating gold particle canvas background (AppBackground), marquee/ticker patterns for rounds and insights.
- RTL Hebrew UI throughout.
- Existing surface language: soft glass panels (subtle white-alpha borders + backdrop blur) floating over the navy/particle background, gold glow for emphasis, green (#4ade80) for "won/correct", red for live/urgent.
- Anti-reference: avoid looking like a generic gray admin/BI dashboard. Should feel like tournament broadcast graphics, not a spreadsheet.

### Design Principles
1. Broadcast-grade drama over utilitarian dashboard — treat numbers and results as moments to present, not just report.
2. Gold is a spotlight, not a wash — use it sparingly for the thing that matters most in a given view (per the app's existing 60/30/10 gold-as-accent usage).
3. Everything is pull, not push — screens must reward a self-initiated visit; no need to design for external notification/preview surfaces.
4. Stay legible at a glance — despite the theatrical tone, a friend checking in for 10 seconds should immediately grasp "what happened" and "where do I stand."
5. RTL-first, dark-first — all layouts and motion must work natively in Hebrew RTL on the existing navy/gold dark theme.
